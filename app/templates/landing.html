When someone leaves, the reasons shouldn’t.

Keep the decisions, context and unfinished work behind a project. Handover ties every claim to the quote it came from, checks that the quote says what the claim says, and leaves publishing to a person.

Reads meeting notes, team conversations, task exports and public GitHub activity. Secrets are masked before anything is stored, and a person approves every claim.

From scattered information to something the next person can trust.

One workflow keeps the source, the claim and the decision connected.

  1. 1

    Collect the context

    Bring in notes, conversations, task exports and public repository activity.

  2. 2

    Check every claim

    Handover extracts decisions, owners, risks and dependencies, links each to its source quote, and flags gaps and contradictions.

  3. 3

    Review and hand over

    A person approves the claims. Approved versions become numbered releases that each role sees in the right detail.

Not just a summary. A trail you can inspect.

Reviewers see the claim, the exact quote behind it, why it got its label and what changed over time. They can approve, edit, reject or reopen it.

  • The riskiest claims come first.
  • Every quote links back to its source.
  • An edited claim is checked again and needs approval again.
Read the demo walkthrough

Four labels. No hidden confidence score.

The checker says what it found. A label is an evidence check, not a guarantee that a statement is true.

quote-verified
The quote exists in the source and passes the mechanical support checks.
inferred
The quote exists, but the claim goes further than the quote does.
conflicting
Source evidence contradicts the claim.
unsupported
No usable quote was found, or no evidence was attached.

AI can help prepare the handover.
It cannot approve it.

Automation organizes project knowledge. It never becomes the authority on what gets shared.

AI is optional

Rule-based extraction works without an API key. Model output is validated and stays a draft.

Access is enforced

Role and source sensitivity decide what each reader sees. Confidential material is withheld from remote models by default.

Releases keep history

Published releases are numbered and fixed. A correction is reviewed and goes out in the next release.
