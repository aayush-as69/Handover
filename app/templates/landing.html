Evidence-linked knowledge handover

Keep critical project knowledge from leaving with people.

Handover turns notes, chat, task exports and public GitHub activity into a handover where every claim points to the exact words it came from. A person reviews and approves it before anyone relies on it.

What the label means

quote-verified The quotes exist in the sources and fit the claim.

inferred The claim goes beyond its quote.

conflicting A source contradicts it.

unsupported No usable quote was found.

Quote-verified is not the same as true. That judgement stays with the reviewer.

1. Collect

Bring in what exists

Meeting notes, team chat, task exports and public GitHub issues, commits and pull requests. Secrets are masked on the way in.

2. Review

Check every claim

Each draft claim shows its quotes, a computed label and its history. Riskiest claims come first. Nothing is approved automatically.

3. Hand over

Publish a release

Approved claims form a numbered, unchangeable release. Each person sees only what their role may see.

Built to be checked

Conflicts and open questions

Handover flags where sources disagree, for example one note saying PostgreSQL is the only datastore while a commit adds Mongo. It also lists what the outgoing person should still answer: owners, reasons and open decisions.

Safe by design

Permissions enforced on the server

Sensitivity levels run from public to confidential, and a claim is as sensitive as its most sensitive source. Restricted material is hidden from roles without clearance, not just greyed out.

Try it

The demo uses an invented project, CampusEvents, so nothing real is exposed. Sign in as a reviewer, open the queue, and approve or reject a claim.
