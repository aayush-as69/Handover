From scattered information to trusted handover

  1. Collect project knowledgeImport notes, conversations, task exports and public GitHub activity. Secrets are masked on the way in.
  2. Extract meaningful informationIdentify decisions, owners, dependencies, unresolved tasks and risks as draft claims.
  3. Verify and reviewConnect each claim to its original quotes, check support and contradictions, and require a person to approve.
  4. Publish the handoverCreate numbered releases with role-based visibility and acknowledgement.

Every claim gets one of four labels

Computed by the checker, never typed in by hand.

quote-verified
The quotes exist in the sources and fit the claim. That is not the same as true; the reviewer still decides.
inferred
The quote exists, but the claim adds a number, a date or a conclusion it does not contain.
conflicting
A quote in the sources contradicts the claim.
unsupported
No usable evidence: the claim has no quote, or none of its quotes can be found in the sources.

AI can help prepare the handover. It cannot approve it.

  • AI extraction is optional. Rule-based extraction works without an API key.
  • Source evidence is checked. Every quote is searched for in the original text before a label is given.
  • Only a person approves. Not even a quote-verified claim is published automatically.
  • Confidential material stays home. Confidential chunks are withheld from remote AI extraction by default.
  • Access follows role and sensitivity. A claim is as sensitive as its most sensitive source, and the server enforces it.
  • Releases keep their versions. Published releases are numbered and fixed; corrections go into the next one.

What Handover preserves

Decisions and the reasons behind them. Who owns what. Risks and blockers. Dependencies. Open questions. Recurring problems. Contacts. Each one stays linked to the words it came from.
